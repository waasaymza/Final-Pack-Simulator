What this run is telling you

    How the line is modeled

    Each Mamata drops pouches onto a short belt into its Loma. Every film and zipper roll runs a set number of pouches, then that Mamata stops for the change. Mamata 2's rolls start half used so the two baggers don't change at the same time. Rejected pouches leave the line at the Loma.

    Good pouches go to that lane's pick station on the BPA, which holds one pick group. If the pick group is already waiting, pouches gather on the belt behind it. Once the belt has gathered its limit, the diverter fires and everything gathered goes to the tote, not just the extra pouch. If the combined pouch feed from the running Mamatas is over the BPA's limit for the current head, the extra pouches over the limit are missed by the smart tracks and go to the tote. At the stall rate the smart tracks can't keep up at all and every pouch goes to the tote. Tote pouches can be hand-fed back through the Loma while that lane's Mamata is stopped.

    The gantry stacks pouches into a 7- or 9-slot cartridge, one pouch per slot each pick, alternating lanes and running on one lane if the other is down. After 7 or 8 picks the stacks are pushed into the cases together. While the gantry loads the next cartridge, the case side pushes the full cases out and pulls in empties from the Lantech.

    Cases can queue before the taper. The taper, case checkweigher, and printers then run as one station at one speed with no buffer between them, and a stop on any of the three stops the station. The ARB feeds cases into the robot at the slower of the ARB and robot speeds. When a pallet is full, the robot pauses for the pallet push-out and the new pallet and slip sheet, either at the same time or one after the other.
